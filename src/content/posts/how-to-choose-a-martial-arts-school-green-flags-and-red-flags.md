Choosing a martial arts school is not like picking a gym membership. You are choosing a teacher, a community, and a set of values that will shape how you or your child trains for years. That decision deserves more than a quick Google search and a flashy website.

Here is what to pay attention to.

## Green Flags: Signs You Are in the Right Place

**The instructor teaches the class.** This sounds obvious, but in many large commercial schools, you sign up based on the head instructor's reputation and then spend most of your time with junior assistants. A good school has an experienced instructor on the floor, paying attention to students, correcting technique, and actually present.

**They are honest about what they teach.** If a school clearly explains its style, its curriculum, and what students can realistically expect to achieve, that is a good sign. Honest schools do not promise you will become a fighter in six weeks or guarantee a black belt on a timeline.

**You can watch a class before you commit.** Any school confident in what it offers will let you sit and observe. Watch how the instructor talks to students, especially beginners and kids. Watch whether students are engaged or just going through motions. That one class tells you more than any brochure.

**The culture respects everyone in the room.** Martial arts training is demanding, but it should never feel humiliating. If you see an instructor belittle a struggling student, or if senior students treat beginners like inconveniences, walk away. Good training is challenging and respectful at the same time.

**There is no pressure to sign a long contract on day one.** Some schools lock you into multi-year agreements before you have even broken a sweat. A school that believes in its training gives you time to decide.

## Red Flags: Slow Down and Ask Questions

**Belt factories.** If a school advertises rapid belt progression or charges a fee every time a student tests, pay attention. Belts should reflect genuine skill and time. A black belt earned in 18 months at a McDojo and one earned after years of real work are not the same thing, no matter what colour the fabric is.

**Guaranteed black belt programs.** This is a red flag dressed up as a selling point. No ethical instructor guarantees a rank in advance. Rank is earned, not purchased.

**Vague answers about lineage or style.** You do not need to become a martial arts historian, but a credible instructor can tell you clearly what style they teach, where they trained, and who they trained under. If the answers are evasive or the credentials are impossible to verify, be cautious.

**High pressure sales tactics.** If the first conversation is more about payment plans than training, something is off. A school focused on its students leads with the training.

**An instructor who discourages questions.** In traditional martial arts, respect for the instructor matters. But respect is different from blind obedience. A good teacher welcomes curious students. If asking a simple question makes you feel unwelcome, trust that feeling.

Trust your instincts when you walk in the door. Does the space feel serious and purposeful? Does the instructor make eye contact and seem genuinely interested in your goals? Do the students seem to actually like being there?

Those small things matter as much as any credential on the wall.
