Have you ever wondered why someone might leave behind something they love? It wasn't an easy decision, but sometimes the best way forward is to create something new.

I left Jitsu-Do Karate in Newmarket to build a dojo that truly embodies my vision of martial arts in 2026.

## **A Change in Philosophy**

When you’re deeply involved in something as dynamic as martial arts, aligning your philosophy with your practice is crucial. Over time, it became clear that my vision had diverged from that of Jitsu-Do.

- **Our Approach:** Jitsu-Do had a traditional framework, focusing heavily on competition and rank progression. While these elements have their place, they started to overshadow what I believed in.
  
- **What Changed for Me:** My focus shifted towards a more holistic approach, prioritizing personal growth over medals. Martial arts should empower people of all backgrounds to become better versions of themselves.

- **The New Focus:** My new dojo practices this philosophy by encouraging students to discover their own paths within martial arts, without heavy emphasis on traditional hierarchies.

## **Nurturing a Community Atmosphere**

Think about the power of community in your own life. At Jitsu-Do, I witnessed how a strong community could inspire and motivate. Yet, there came a point where the structure didn’t support the community I envisioned.

- **Why It Mattered:** Classes often felt packed, with little room for personal connection or truly understanding our students’ unique needs.

- **Building Connections:** I wanted a dojo where everyone knows your name—literally. So, we keep class sizes small, ensuring more personalized attention and genuine relationships.

- **Community First:** We host regular events to foster friendships, not just among students, but their families as well. It’s these connections that make our practice space more than just a dojo—it’s a second home.

## **Innovating Training Techniques**

Sticking to tradition has its merits, but evolution is just as important. At Jitsu-Do, I felt a struggle to integrate innovative training methods which acknowledge how society and technology are evolving.

- **The Traditional vs. Modern Debate:** While traditional techniques are valuable, the martial arts world is continuously advancing. I wanted to incorporate both timeless wisdom and modern innovations.

- **Embracing Technology:** Our dojo now uses technology to enhance training. Video analysis helps students see their progress in real time, offering feedback that’s immediate and impactful.

- **Well-Rounded Training:** By experimenting with cross-disciplinary techniques such as mindfulness and fitness testing, our dojo supports better physical and mental agility.

## **Creating a Sanctuary for Everyone**

This might surprise you, but not every dojo is welcoming to everyone. Across my time in martial arts, diversity in students was often more a checkbox than a commitment.

- **Diversity Beyond Buzzwords:** I believe everyone deserves a place where they feel safe and included, irrespective of age, gender, or ability.

- **Ensuring Accessibility:** To genuinely make martial arts accessible, we’ve implemented sliding scale fees and adaptive programs. We focus on inclusivity because everyone should have the chance to discover what martial arts can do for them.

- **A Welcoming Space:** Our dojo strives to eliminate barriers—financial, physical, or emotional—to ensure that our space is as welcoming as possible.

## **Finding Meaning in Each Step**

Walking away from Jitsu-Do was not simply a career move; it was a path to deeper meaning—a realization that life’s real dojo is everywhere you look. Every experience is an opportunity to learn and grow.

- **Continuous Growth:** My new venture has taught me the importance of learning from every interaction and challenge, emphasizing growth over achievement.

- **Looking Forward:** By focusing on values that matter—such as integrity, respect, and empathy—I’ve built a dojo that aligns not only with what I believe martial arts can be but what it should be in today’s world.

Thank you for taking this journey with me. Leaving Jitsu-Do was challenging, but it led to a dojo built on these principles: connecting, innovating, and including. Come visit us, and let’s grow together.

Warm regards,
Shihan Michael Ginn
