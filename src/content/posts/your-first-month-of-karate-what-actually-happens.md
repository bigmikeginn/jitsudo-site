Starting something new is uncomfortable. That is the honest truth, and it is worth saying up front.

Your first month of karate is not going to feel smooth. You will forget which foot goes where. You will punch with the wrong hand. You will look around the room to copy what everyone else is doing, and that is completely fine. Every single person on that floor has been exactly where you are.

Here is what actually happens when you walk through the door at Jitsu-Do Karate in Newmarket.

## Week One: You Are Just Finding Your Feet

The first class is mostly about orientation. Shihan Ginn or one of the senior students will show you how the class runs — how to bow in, where to stand, how to address an instructor. None of it is complicated, but it is different from anything you have done before, and different takes a little time to sink in.

You will learn your first basic stances and maybe your first couple of strikes. Your body will feel stiff and a little awkward. That is normal. You are asking your muscles to move in ways they have never moved before.

Expect to feel a little lost. Expect to feel a little tired. Go home, drink some water, and come back.

## Week Two: The Repetition Starts to Mean Something

By the second week, you will notice something small but real: the warm-up does not feel quite as foreign. You remember how to line up. You know roughly what a front stance looks like, even if yours still needs work.

This is where the repetition starts to do its job. Karate is built on basics drilled over and over until they stop being something you think about and start being something you just do. In week two, you are nowhere near that point — but you can feel the process starting.

You might also start to notice the other students around you. The white belts who showed up a month before you. The colour belts who move with a quiet confidence. That confidence is not a personality trait. It is just accumulated practice, and it is available to anyone willing to put in the time.

## Week Three: The First Wall

Week three is often where beginners hit their first real frustration. The initial excitement has worn off. Progress feels slow. A technique you thought you had figured out last week suddenly feels wrong again.

This is not a sign that karate is not for you. It is a sign that you are actually learning something real.

Shihan Ginn talks about this directly with new students. The uncomfortable plateau is part of the process, not an obstacle to it. Pushing through it — not by training harder necessarily, but by training honestly and consistently — is where real growth starts.

Show up anyway. That is the whole answer.

## Week Four: You Belong There

By the end of your first month, something has quietly shifted. You walk in and you know what to do. You recognize faces. You have a sense of the culture of the dojo — the respect, the focus, the moments of humour that make hard training feel less heavy.

You are not advanced. You are not even close to advanced. But you are no longer a stranger, and the basics that felt impossible four weeks ago now feel like yours.

You will also notice that your body is responding differently. Better posture. A little more awareness of how you are standing or moving. Small things, but real ones.

## What to Bring and What to Leave at the Door

You do not need any gear for your first class — just comfortable clothes you can move in and bare feet. Leave your ego at the door, genuinely. The fastest learners are almost always the ones who are willing to look awkward, ask questions, and not take themselves too seriously.

Karate rewards patience and consistency above everything else. One month is not a long time. But it is enough time to find out whether this practice has something to offer you — and for most people who stick it out, it does.
