Signing up for a new class is the easy part. Walking through the door for the first time is where most people hesitate. That's completely normal, and it happens to almost everyone who starts training Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu. So let's just talk about what actually happens when you arrive, step by step.

## When You Walk In

You don't need a gi for your very first class. Come in comfortable athletic wear — shorts or leggings, a t-shirt, bare feet on the mat. If you do eventually stick with BJJ, you'll want to pick up a gi, but don't let gear stop you from showing up.

Shihan Ginn or one of the instructors will greet you, get your name, and give you a quick rundown of how class works. You won't be thrown into the deep end. Nobody expects you to know anything walking in, and nobody is going to make you feel bad for being a beginner. That's just not how we run things at Jitsu-Do.

## The Warm-Up

Class typically opens with a warm-up that's meant to get your body moving and introduce you to how the mat feels under you. Expect some light movement drills — shrimping (a sliding hip-escape motion), forward and backward rolls, and basic positional movements. These aren't just warm-up filler. They're the foundational movements of BJJ, and you'll keep doing them for years.

If something hurts or you have an injury, speak up before class starts. Good instructors want to know, and there's no judgment.

## The Technique Portion

After the warm-up, the instructor demonstrates a technique — usually a guard pass, a sweep, a submission, or an escape. This is where most of the class time goes. You'll be paired with a partner and practice the move repeatedly, back and forth.

As a new student, you'll likely be paired with someone more experienced who can walk you through it. Don't stress about doing it perfectly. The whole point of drilling is repetition, and nobody gets it clean on the first try. Pay attention to the big picture — the concept behind the move — rather than trying to memorize every detail at once.

## Rolling (Sparring)

At the end of class, more experienced students will do live sparring, which BJJ people call rolling. As a brand-new student, you may watch this portion or you may be invited to do some very light positional work with a patient training partner. Either way, nobody is going to put you in a tough spot on your first day.

What you'll notice watching others roll is that it looks much calmer than a movie fight scene. Good BJJ is methodical and controlled. People tap (signal submission) quickly and reset without drama. The culture on the mat in Newmarket, as in most good gyms, is that your training partners are your biggest asset — you don't try to hurt them.

## What You'll Actually Feel After Class

Honestly? Tired, a little sore in places you didn't know existed, and probably curious enough to want to come back. Your brain will feel full. You'll have used muscles you forgot you had. And somewhere in there, you'll have done something real — you tried something genuinely difficult.

BJJ has a learning curve. There's no shortcut around it. But the first class is just about getting a feel for the environment and moving your body in new ways. Everything else comes with time on the mat.

## A Few Practical Things

- Trim your fingernails and toenails before class. This is a courtesy to your training partners.
- Bring water.
- Leave your ego at the door — not as a cliché, but as a genuine strategy. You'll learn faster.
- Ask questions. The instructors are there to teach, not just demonstrate.
